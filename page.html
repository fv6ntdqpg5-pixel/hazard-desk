Warnings on the map, radar and forecast outlooks cover the United States only, because no free worldwide equivalent exists; storms, earthquakes, volcanoes, wildfires and tsunami messages cover the world. "Your spot" gives the official forecast and alerts in the United States and Canada, and a labeled computer model forecast everywhere else. For warnings where you are, follow your national weather and civil protection services.

Right now

    Sources, United States and North America. Local alerts, current conditions and the hourly and 7-day forecast for your spot: the National Weather Service, read by your browser from api.weather.gov. NOAA National Hurricane Center and Central Pacific Hurricane Center (storms, forecast cones, tropical outlooks), NWS (warnings and watches), Storm Prediction Center (severe storm, tornado and fire weather outlooks), NOAA MRMS radar, Iowa Environmental Mesonet (storm reports), Weather Prediction Center (excessive rainfall and heavy snow outlooks), USGS Earthquake Hazards Program, and the USGS volcano observatories. For a spot in Canada: the city forecast, current conditions and alerts from Environment and Climate Change Canada, read by your browser from api.weather.gc.ca (Contains information licensed under the Data Server End-use Licence of Environment and Climate Change Canada). Canadian and Mexican warnings are not drawn on the map.

    Sources, world. Tropical storms outside the Hurricane Center's area, floods and volcano alerts: GDACS, the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System of the United Nations and the European Commission, with storm tracks from the national and joint warning centers. Erupting volcanoes: the Smithsonian Institution's Global Volcanism Program. Earthquakes outside North America: USGS, magnitude 4.5 and up. Tsunami messages: NOAA's tsunami warning centers. Sea temperature: NASA GIBS, MUR daily analysis from JPL. Wildfires: the EU Global Wildfire Information System via GDACS. Space weather and aurora: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Caldera status: USGS, GeoNet (New Zealand), INGV (Italy) and the Smithsonian. Local forecast outside the United States and Canada: a computer model, Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0), not an agency forecast. Town names for "Your spot" are from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0).

    A web page only helps while it is open. Keep Wireless Emergency Alerts turned on in your phone and have a second way to get warnings, such as a NOAA Weather Radio. The NWS feed itself can run a minute or two behind a new warning.

    Not an official warning service. This is an independent summary. For decisions about life and property, follow your local National Weather Service office at weather.gov, the National Hurricane Center at nhc.noaa.gov, and local emergency officials. A tsunami message from NOAA's centers is advice to national authorities outside the United States; each country decides its own warnings. Storm wind zones outside the Hurricane Center's area are GDACS estimates, not official forecast cones.
