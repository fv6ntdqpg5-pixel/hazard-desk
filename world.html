              Supervolcanoes and giant calderas

              A supervolcano is one that has had a magnitude 8 eruption (VEI 8, more than 1,000 cubic km). Three below have; the VEI 7 calderas are included because they are the same kind of system and are often called supervolcanoes. Status comes live from the agency that watches each one. None of these is a forecast.

                Sources. Tropical storms, floods and volcano alerts: GDACS, the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System of the United Nations and the European Commission, with storm tracks from the national and joint warning centers. Erupting volcanoes: the Smithsonian Institution's Global Volcanism Program. U.S. volcano alert levels: USGS. Earthquakes: USGS, magnitude 4.5 and up. Tsunami messages: NOAA's tsunami warning centers. Sea temperature: NASA GIBS, MUR daily analysis from JPL. Wildfires: the EU Global Wildfire Information System via GDACS. Space weather and aurora: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Caldera status: USGS, GeoNet (New Zealand), INGV (Italy) and the Smithsonian.

                Not an official warning service. This page summarizes public data and only helps while it is open. A tsunami message from NOAA's centers is advice to national authorities outside the United States; each country decides its own warnings. Storm wind zones are GDACS estimates, not official forecast cones.
